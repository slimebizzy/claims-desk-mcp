# Claims Handling Guidelines (Internal)

Cobalt Harbor Mutual Insurance Company (fictional). Internal use only. Not for distribution to policyholders. Illustrative content written for a portfolio project.

## 1. First Notice of Loss

### 1.1 Contact standard
The assigned adjuster must make first contact with the insured within 24 hours of first notice of loss (FNOL) for property claims and within 4 business hours for any claim involving bodily injury. Document the contact attempt in the claim file even if unsuccessful.

### 1.2 Coverage verification
Before discussing coverage with the insured, verify that the policy was in force on the date of loss, the loss location matches the residence premises or a covered auto, and premium is not past the cancellation date. Apply any state endorsement in force for the loss location before applying the base form.

### 1.3 Reservation of rights
If facts suggest a coverage question, such as a possible exclusion or late notice, issue a reservation of rights letter within 10 business days of identifying the issue. Do not deny coverage by phone.

## 2. Reserve Authority

### 2.1 Authority levels
Adjusters may set and change reserves only within their authority level:
- Level 1 (Associate Adjuster): up to $25,000 per claim.
- Level 2 (Senior Adjuster): up to $100,000 per claim.
- Level 3 (Claims Supervisor): up to $250,000 per claim.
- Above $250,000: Regional Claims Manager approval is required.

### 2.2 Reserve review
Reserves must be reviewed every 30 days and whenever material new information is received. A reserve change of more than 50% in a single adjustment requires a file note explaining the reason.

### 2.3 Catastrophe claims
During a declared catastrophe, Level 1 authority is temporarily raised to $50,000 for windstorm and hail claims, unless the claim involves suspected fraud.

## 3. Special Investigations Unit Referral

### 3.1 Fraud indicators
Refer the claim to the Special Investigations Unit (SIU) within 5 business days if two or more of the following indicators are present:
- The policy was purchased or coverage increased within 60 days before the loss.
- The insured is unusually familiar with claims procedures or insurance terminology.
- The insured pressures for a quick settlement or offers to accept a lower amount in cash.
- Receipts or documents appear altered, or the insured cannot produce proof of ownership for high-value items.
- The loss occurs shortly after the insured disclosed financial difficulty.
- Multiple prior claims of a similar type within 36 months.

### 3.2 Handling while under review
While a claim is under SIU review, continue to handle the claim in good faith and meet all regulatory deadlines. Do not disclose the SIU referral to the insured.

## 4. Settlement Practices

### 4.1 Water damage and mold
For water damage claims, confirm whether the leak was sudden or continuous. Continuous seepage over 14 or more days is excluded under HO-3 Section 4.1, but resulting mold may still be covered up to the $10,000 fungi sublimit when the water damage itself is covered. Photograph moisture readings at the first inspection.

### 4.2 Depreciation holdback
On replacement cost claims, pay actual cash value first and record the depreciation holdback. Release the holdback after receiving proof that repair or replacement is complete, provided the request falls within the policy's time limit for claiming replacement cost.

### 4.3 Appraisal requests
If the insured invokes appraisal, acknowledge the request in writing within 5 business days and name the company appraiser within the 20-day window in HO-3 Section 6.5.
